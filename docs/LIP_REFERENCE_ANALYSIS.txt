REFERENCE: Morning_English_A_Rose_in_the_Garden (1).mp4
Duration: 20.000 seconds; 1920x1080; 24fps; H.264 + AAC + subtitles.
Analysis was performed from the user-provided video for this project.

OBSERVED REFERENCE MOTION
- Wide establishing shot first, then cuts into different speaker-focused medium shots.
- The talking character's mouth visibly shifts from resting/closed to open
  and rounded speech shapes. Lip shapes are not merely a stretched smile.
- Non-speaking people generally keep a relaxed mouth expression.
- Eyes and heads have restrained motion; body movement is secondary.
- Subtitles remain at the bottom after the camera changes.

OUR PRIOR DEFECTS
- Old renderer warped the same original smiling pixels in a tiny ROI.
  Its six labels did not correspond to clearly different mouth openings.
- Camera remained wide, reducing the mouth to a few screen pixels.
- Most dialogue was visually indistinguishable from a still illustration.

CHANGES
- Introduced mouth_rig.py with distinct closed, soft, E, A, O and F/V
  mouth variants, retaining transparent sprites and colored watercolor lips.
- Pose switching is driven by Kokoro timed phonemes (not audio amplitude).
- Three-frame smooth transitions retained; only current speaker's lips move.
- Every 10-second location gets a wide establishing and closing shot, with
  medium character close-ups during dialogue. Scenes remain 10 seconds long.
- The renderer keeps eye-blink/gaze and short joke reactions.

LIMITATIONS / ACCEPTANCE
- These are procedural sprite-based mouth illustrations; this is not
  pixel-identical reproduction of third-party imagery or neural full-face
  performance capture. Artist-painted aligned phoneme sprites remain the
  optimal next quality step.
- Human review against this reference is essential: confirm mouth seams,
  eye motion, pronunciation, and speaker timing through the whole video.
- Run unit tests and render a 20-second preview before the full two minutes.
- Large rendered media should be distributed as GitHub Releases, not Git blobs.
